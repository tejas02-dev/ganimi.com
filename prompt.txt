You are a senior product designer + UX writer + frontend engineer.

You are building a single-page, scroll-based landing website for a SaaS platform that helps students discover the right career path by tracking their talent, interest, and passion over many years.

The platform connects students, parents, and vendors (coaching centers, mentors, institutes). Vendors log student performance, feedback, and progress. Over time, this data reveals patterns that help guide career decisions.

The target audience is parents, not students.

The website must NOT look like a traditional SaaS page.
No feature lists.
No dashboards.
No technical jargon (no “AI”, “ERP”, “analytics”, “algorithms”).

🧠 Core Philosophy (Implicit, not stated directly)

Most people are dissatisfied with their careers because their talent, interest, and passion were never connected.

Parents don’t lack care — they lack visibility over time.

Career clarity is not a one-time test. It is built slowly through observation.

The website should make parents feel this truth without explaining it directly.

🧩 Page Structure Requirements

Single vertical scrolling page

Each section represents a stage in a child’s life

Each stage contains:

A short emotional statement

ONE powerful question directed at the parent

Very minimal text per section

Strong use of whitespace

Smooth scroll transitions

Calm, trustworthy tone (not flashy, not corporate)

🧒 Life Stages & Questions (Must Be Included)
1. Birth / Early Life

Theme: Infinite potential

Question:

“What will your child be good at?”

2. Early Childhood (Play & Curiosity)

Theme: Exploration

Question:

“Which of these is just play — and which is a signal?”

3. School Years (Marks & Comparison)

Theme: Confusion between scores and ability

Question:

“Are marks showing ability… or just memory?”

4. Teenage Years (Identity & Interest)

Theme: Changing interests

Question:

“Is your child losing interest — or discovering it?”

5. Subject / Stream Selection

Theme: Pressure vs insight

Question:

“Is this choice based on understanding… or pressure?”

6. Coaching, Courses & Certifications

Theme: Too many inputs, no clarity

Question:

“Do you know which of these is actually helping your child?”

7. Early Career / Adult Life

Theme: Late realization

Question:

“What if this confusion started years ago?”

🌱 Final Reveal Section (Very Subtle)

Do NOT introduce the product early

The reveal should feel like a natural conclusion, not a sales pitch

Key ideas to communicate gently:

Talent, interest, and passion leave patterns over time

Decisions don’t have to be guesses

Clarity can be built, not forced

Suggested tone:

Reflective. Calm. Reassuring.

🔘 Call To Action (Important)

CTA must feel safe and low-commitment

No urgency language

No pricing or demos

Acceptable CTA examples:

“Track your child’s journey”

“See how clarity is built over time”

“Understand your child beyond marks”

CTA should lead to:

Email capture OR

Early access / waitlist signup

🎨 Visual & Interaction Guidelines

Soft, emotionally neutral color palette

Each life stage can subtly change background tone

Simple illustrations or symbolic visuals (not cartoons)

Smooth easing and scroll-based animations

Mobile-first design

No loud gradients, no aggressive branding

🧪 Output Expectations from You (AI Agent)

You should produce:

Complete page layout structure

Exact copy text for each section

Component breakdown (hero sections, scroll sections, CTA)

Frontend implementation (React / Next.js preferred)

Smooth scrolling and subtle animations

Clean, production-ready code

🚫 Explicitly Avoid

Feature lists

Pricing tables

Testimonials (at this stage)

Technical explanations

Fear-based language

Corporate buzzwords

✅ Success Criteria

A parent scrolling the page should think:

“I never looked at my child’s journey this way.”

Only after that realization should they feel comfortable signing up.
